About & methodology

Why thermal models need more than a mesh

A thermal camera does not see colour. It sees how much infrared a surface gives off, which depends on its temperature and on what it is made of. A model that looks right in daylight can look completely wrong in infrared.

What infrared simulation is

Infrared simulation renders what a thermal camera would record: a long-wave camera (8–14 µm) for most vehicles and people, mid-wave (3–5 µm) for hot exhausts. Engineers use the images to compare sensors before buying them, and to train and test perception software without flying real aircraft or driving real cars at night.

Why parts and materials matter

A car body, its windscreen, its tyres and its exhaust are at different temperatures and shed heat differently. Glass is opaque in the long-wave band, bare metal reflects the sky instead of glowing, and rubber glows brightly. A single merged mesh with one texture cannot capture any of that, so each model here is split into the components the real object has, and each component gets its own material.

How an entry is made

  1. Licence check. Only CC0, CC-BY, CC-BY-SA and the contributor's own work are accepted. Anything else is turned away before any work is done.
  2. Identify the real object and look up its published dimensions, so the model is scaled to metres.
  3. Split into functional parts such as motors, battery, exhaust and glazing, including hidden ones like an engine inside a car shell.
  4. Research each part's material and its long-wave emissivity.
  5. Record the source of every value as measured, reference (from a published table) or estimated.
  6. Publish with credit: the author, the original link, the licence and a note of what was changed.

How far to trust the numbers

Emissivity depends on surface finish, paint, ageing, wavelength and viewing angle. The values here are starting points for a simulation, not laboratory measurements of the specific object, unless an entry says measured. Check the source tag on each part.

Where files live

This site is a static page set on GitHub Pages: images, descriptions and the catalogue. The large model files (.blend, .usdc, .fbx, .glb) are hosted on free storage such as GitHub Releases, Hugging Face or Zenodo, and each model page links to them.
